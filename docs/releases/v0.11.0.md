# Second Nature 0.11.0: Restoration Warden

**Stable Factorio 2.0.77 with Space Age, Quality and Elevated Rails. Alpha prerelease.**

Download **`second-nature_0.11.0.zip`**, keep it zipped in the Factorio `mods`
folder, and replace older Second Nature copies after backing up saves. Use the
named mod ZIP, not GitHub's automatic source archive. A matching SHA-256 file is
attached. Private repository downloads require GitHub sign-in.

**Existing saves upgrade in place.** No recipe, research cost, power rating,
footprint or ecological rate changed. This release is character art and file
size only.

**The download is 40% smaller: 114.1 MB to 68.7 MB.**

## A character that belongs in this mod

The player character has been rebuilt from scratch. The previous explorer model,
its rig, its styling module and all twenty-four of its atlases are deleted.

Second Nature is a mod about working inside a poisoned atmosphere - scrubbers,
precipitation towers, direct air capture, respirator-grade industry. The old
character was an unmasked figure in a short skirt with bare arms and legs,
styled after a fashion reference that had nothing to do with the subject. It
fought the fiction of every machine standing next to it.

The replacement is a **sealed restoration warden**. The silhouette is built from
three shapes chosen because they survive being drawn at roughly 33 pixels wide:

- a **hooded work parka**, which reads as cloth and gives a soft flared outline
  distinct from the mod's hard-edged machinery;
- a **full-face respirator** with a blunt snout and cheek filters - the reason
  this character can stand in its own pollution;
- a **back seed hopper** of graded stock with a germination lamp, so the
  character carries the mod's actual goal on its back.

A broad **emissive visor band** keeps the warden legible on unlit night terrain
and makes its facing obvious at normal zoom.

Nothing renders bare skin, hair or a face. That is a deliberate fiction choice,
and a test enforces it.

### Three armour appearances, not three recolours

| Tier | Parka | Accent | Added geometry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field gear | olive drab | oxidised copper | - |
| Expedition gear | slate green | teal | right pauldron, second canister |
| Bastion gear | cold blue-grey | pale cyan | both pauldrons, upper plates, hood crown, helmet lamp |

### Nothing about how it moves changed

The warden is a drop-in replacement for the renderer and the engine. It keeps
the same skeleton, the same poses and frame counts, the same eighteen-row armed
layout, the same pickaxe grip contract (right hand at 48% of the shaft, left at
16%) and the same 1.58-tile projected height. Locomotion, aiming and mining
behave exactly as before, and the startup toggle still restores the vanilla or
another mod's character without touching inventories or controllers.

## 114.1 MB to 68.7 MB

Sprite data was 94% of the download, and the old character alone was 36.5 MB of
it - 32% of everything shipped, for a figure occupying about 33x64 screen
pixels. Nearly all of that went into a sculpted face, individual hair strands
and per-limb tattoos: high-frequency colour variation, which is precisely what
PNG cannot compress.

Every optimisation candidate was measured on this mod's own art rather than
assumed, and two of the obvious ones turned out to be traps:

- **Cropping the transparent padding returns 0.3-1.4%.** The character sheets
  are only 9-17% non-transparent, which looks like an enormous win, but fully
  transparent regions already collapse to almost nothing under DEFLATE.
- **Non-integer downscaling makes files three times larger.** Resampling a
  7,000-colour sheet by 1.5x produced 40,000 colours of interpolation noise.

What actually costs bytes is colour variety. Sprites are now quantised in colour
while **every alpha channel is copied back verbatim**, so antialiased
silhouettes and cast shadows are bit-identical and only invisible colour
precision is spent. A lossless `oxipng` pass follows. Ordinary palette indexing
was rejected despite being the biggest win: it cut the sprites' 74 alpha levels
to 6 and visibly hardened every edge.

**No sprite lost a single pixel of resolution, a frame, or a turret direction.**
Measured error on visible pixels is under 1.2 of 255 (PSNR 38-40 dB), and alpha
is unchanged.

The warden is authored for the same goal from the other direction: ten flat
tones per tier, broad uninterrupted panels, roughly half the geometry of the
model it replaces, and a rasterizer finish that omits the fine surface grain
used on machinery - invisible at character scale, expensive to encode.

### Deliberately left alone

- **Audio.** 6.5 MB, already at 60-110 kbps stereo and 58-61 kbps mono.
  Re-encoding saves 1-2 MB at most and risks the working soundscape.
- **Machine, turret, train and power-plant resolution.** These store 90-107
  pixels per tile against vanilla's 65-71. Halving them would put the mod
  visibly below the base game.
- **Frame counts and turret directions.** 16-20 frames and 64 directions match
  vanilla convention; reducing them would produce visible judder.

## Verification

318 tests pass, and the headless 2.0.77 engine job loads the mod with the new
character and the re-encoded sprites. New guards cover the sealed fiction, the
palette budget, the geometry budget, the distinct tier palettes, and that every
shipped PNG stays true RGBA - `oxipng` will otherwise silently rewrite any
sheet of 256 colours or fewer as an indexed image, which the engine's atlas
builder rejects.

In-client appearance is a rendered review, not a claim from a played session.
